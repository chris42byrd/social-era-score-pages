Coaching Feedback

Chris did an excellent job mapping the prospect's previously identified problems to the three pillars of the offer and consistently checking for understanding during the pitch. However, when transitioning to the financial model, the ROI calculation became convoluted because the ad spend and the service fee were introduced separately from the labor costs that Joel would incur to fulfill the extra work. When Joel objected to the math because it cuts into his margin, Chris argued the mechanics of the math rather than pausing to unpack Joel's fear of scaling. Instead of debating the calculation, Chris should ask clarifying questions about Joel's operational bottleneck to make him realize that hiring help is the exact necessary step to grow, re-aligning the ROI with the long-term goal of stepping away from the tools.

What Went Well

  • Systematically tied the prospect's problems directly to the 3 pillars of the offer structure.
  • Set a clear agenda and confirmed the core problems and goals at the very beginning of the call.
  • Actively checked for understanding and buy-in throughout the screen-share pitch phase.

What to Do Differently

  • Simplify the ROI calculation presentation; do not get into an argumentative debate over math when the prospect becomes confused.
  • Ask for explicit permission before transitioning into the pitch to ensure the prospect is leaning in and ready to listen.
  • Handle operational scaling objections (like profit margin dropping when hiring) by isolating the fear and tying it back to the ultimate goal of stepping out of day-to-day labor.
