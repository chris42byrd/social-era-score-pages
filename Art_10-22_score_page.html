Coaching Feedback

Chris did an excellent job adapting to the prospect's mood and situation, pivoting away from a typical drawn-out discovery when he sensed Art just wanted the numbers. He demonstrated strong authority by refusing to lower the price when Art asked for a discount. However, he missed opportunities to dig into the cost of inaction and the financial pain of not having a lead structure. In future calls, even when fast-tracking the pitch, quickly anchor the price against what continuing to rely on a shifting real estate market will cost them in lost revenue.

What Went Well

  • Recognizing prospect fatigue and adapting the call structure to respect their time by jumping to the bottom line.
  • Clearly breaking down the expected KPIs (Cost Per Lead, Cost Per Appointment) which built immediate trust and differentiated you from competitors.
  • Holding firm on pricing with a direct 'No' when the prospect asked for a $4,000 budget, displaying strong conviction.

What to Do Differently

  • Explicitly ask for permission before diving into the pitch and pricing breakdown, rather than just stating what you are going to do.
  • Even in a condensed call, briefly ask what happens if they don't fix the problem to establish the cost of inaction and build urgency.
  • Prompt the prospect with 'Where do we go from here?' at the end of the pitch to let them guide the next steps.
